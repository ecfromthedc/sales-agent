## §1 — Major Frameworks (the spine — pull verbatim where useful)

### 1.1 The 3 Moats (Module 1)

> "You're not chasing a moment. You're building a moat."

Three moats, all required, all reinforce each other:
1. **Formats** — the 5 repeatable containers (see §1.5)
2. **Sound** — the optimized sound campaign that puts UGC creators on your audio
3. **Conversion** — one clear next step (save, stream, ticket, join) and posts that move people there

**Carousel angles:**
- Editorial anchor: *"One viral hit isn't a career. A moat is."*
- Value 4-slide: *"Why your last viral moment didn't compound." Hook → 3 Moats explained → Domino sequence (§1.6) → CTA*

### 1.2 The Mindset Shift (Module 1)

> "View the app as a video game. Games have rules, mechanics, patterns. You learn them."

Core reframe: results-focused = trap. Process-focused = compounding. Failure is feedback, not a verdict.

**Lift-ready phrases (Eric's words):**
- *"Get out of your own way and into your algorithm."*
- *"Post it, then improve it."*
- *"Ego out. Data in."*

**Carousel angle:** Editorial anchor — Eric's mindset shift line. Or Value 4-slide on the trap of treating views as a verdict.

### 1.3 The Hero Section (Module 1)

The 10–30 seconds of a song that releases dopamine — the part strangers stop scrolling for. Distinct from the *content hook* (the words). A sound campaign is built around the hero section.

> "If you don't give the internet a chance to come across your sound, you don't have a song. You have an upload."

**4 moving parts of a strong sound campaign:**
1. Hero section
2. Viral content driven by niche resonance
3. Relatable word prompts that condition the audio's trend patterns
4. Weekly repetition + seeding + iteration

**Carousel angles:**
- Value 4-slide: *"You don't have a song. You have an upload." Hook → what a hero section is → 4 parts of a sound campaign → CTA "send us the song"*
- Editorial: the upload line as a single anchor

### 1.4 The 10 Viral Ingredients (Module 2, expanded in Module 6)

The whole ingredient ladder, with the example hooks Eric documented:

| # | Ingredient | Primary signal | Eric's example hook |
|---|---|---|---|

### 1.5 The 5 Formats (Modules 1, 2, 4, 6)

| Format | Best for | Ingredient stack | Core mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Performance** | likes, shares, reposts | universal theme + payoff promise | Content hook → hero section |
| **Transition** | retention, rewatch, shares | transformation + payoff promise + ASMR | Front end (tension) → Transition (dopamine) → Back end (release). "Like a tennis serve." |
| **Carousel** | saves, comments, shares-to-story | payoff promise + suspense (+ value) | Complex → Suspense → Resolve. **Slide 1 = problem. Last slide = payoff.** |
| **Digital Postcard** | DM shares, reposts, shares-to-story | universal theme + ASMR + exclusive (optional) | Living memes — one visual world, one line, hero section underneath |
| **Reaction** | curiosity, comments, retention | memetic desire + payoff promise | Hook → real emotion → payoff. *"Don't manufacture reactions. Capture real ones."* |

**Format decision tree (Module 4):**
- Just starting? → Performance
- Strong hero section? → Transition
- Want high saves? → Digital Postcard or Carousel
- Building proof? → Reaction

**Golden Format concept:** Test 2 formats widely. The one that consistently wins is your Golden Format. Double down. (Modules 1, 2)

**Carousel angles:**
- 7-slide deep dive: one slide per format + a "find your golden" close
- Editorial: *"The goal isn't variety. The goal is clarity. Always double down on what's working."*

### 1.7 Three-Phase Testing (Module 3)

| Phase | Pool size | What's being measured | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Initial | 300–3,000 (first hour) | Did people stop scrolling? Stay past the hook? Watch to the end? | Weak signal = test ends. Strong = Phase 2. |
| 2 — Expansion | 1,000–10,000 | Rewatch %, shares, watch time vs similar, saves | Strong signal = Phase 3 |
| 3 — Scale | FYP at scale | Continued performance vs decay | Algorithm identified pattern; serves to strangers matching it |

> "You can't skip phases. You earn them."

**Account-level trust:** Consistent winning with a golden format → posts skip Phase 1, enter Phase 2 directly. Faster compounding.

**Carousel angle:** Value 4-slide on why the FYP isn't a slot machine — Hook ("You're not unlucky. You're untested") → Build (3 phases) → Payoff (account-level trust) → CTA.

### 1.9 The Flywheel (Module 3)

> "Content lifts sound. Sound lifts content. Social lifts streaming. Streaming lifts social."

10-step loop, paraphrased:
1. Post artist-centric content (the 5 formats)
2. Content performs → earns algorithm trust
3. Sound tag visible → "Add to Like Song" presses
4. Saves sync to Spotify/Apple → popularity score grows
5. Post usage-first content (inviting UGC)
6. Creators use the sound → platform sees trend-worthy
7. UGC = "billboards on the highway of the internet"
8. More audio context → more saves
9. Popularity score crosses thresholds → Discover Weekly, Radio unlock
10. Streaming distribution drives awareness back to social

**Carousel angles:**
- Editorial: *"Content lifts sound. Sound lifts content. Social lifts streaming. Streaming lifts social."* — perfect grid anchor.
- Value 4-slide: the loop laid out — *"Run it long enough and growth becomes inevitable."*

### 2.2 Hooks already used inside course examples

See §1.4 table — one canonical hook per ingredient.

### 2.3 Negative hooks vs positive hooks (Module 6)

- **Negative hooks** create transformation through a perception flip (doubt → awe). Contrast amplifies emotional payoff. Goldford's *"Nobody wants to hear an old man sing soul"* is the canonical example. Pair with the Transformation ingredient.
- **Positive hooks** lead with universal themes and shareability. Less dramatic payoff but pairs with strong CTAs.

### 2.4 Hook-finding method (Module 1)

> "Good hooks are often discovered, not invented. Mine top comments for phrasing."

Process (verbatim from Module 1):
1. Start with the emotion or situation, not the song
2. Search emotion + a format keyword on TikTok
3. Filter by most-liked, note their first line
4. Read top comments — best hook often sitting there
5. Rip the words, rewrite in your voice
6. Test like a scientist: same hero section, 5 hooks/week

## §6 — Voice & Style Notes (the calibration layer)

### 6.1 Eric's repeating metaphors

Pull these — they're calibrated and on-brand:

- *"Tier 1 gets the performance. Tier 2 gets the process. Tier 3 gets the person."*
- *"You don't have a song. You have an upload."*

### 6.2 Sentence cadence (pulled from course)

- Subject-verb-object. Short.
- One declarative line, then one explanation.
- Comparisons via "X is not Y. It's Z." used sparingly — overused this becomes the AI-obvious triplet smell (see HANDOFF §8.2). Use ONCE per carousel, max.
- Closes often turn into a single short imperative or assertion (*"Growth becomes inevitable."* / *"Clarity compounds."*)

### 6.3 Banned phrasing (instant reject)

- *"In today's algorithm-driven landscape…"*
- *"Did you know…"*
- *"Here are 5 tips…"*
- *"This changes everything."*
- *"Elevate your…"*
- *"Stop doing X, start doing Y"* triplets
- *"Most artists…"* opener (overused; only use if the immediate follow-up is concrete)

### 6.4 Voice rules pulled from the course (apply to every carousel)

- *"If you can't name why it worked, you can't repeat it."*
- *"The goal isn't variety. The goal is clarity. Always double down on what's working."*
- *"You don't need to be louder. You need to be clearer. Clarity compounds."*
- *"The algorithm rewards what people can't stop watching. Make it clear. Make it satisfying. Make it repeatable."*

These can be lifted verbatim as carousel Payoff lines.
