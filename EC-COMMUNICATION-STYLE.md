# Eric Cromartie -- Communication Style Profile

## Executive Summary

Eric operates in two distinct registers that blend together depending on audience. With clients and labels, he is warm but economical -- short sentences, no filler, leads with confidence and specific numbers, closes with energy. Internally with his team, he is loose, slang-heavy, and motivational -- "gng," "twin," "let's cook," "gangsta." The through-line in both registers is the same: he moves fast, he leads with what he can do (not what he can't), and he treats every interaction as a chance to build momentum.

His selling philosophy is **relationship-first with challenger instincts**. He does not hard-sell. He shows up with genuine enthusiasm for the music, drops proof points casually, and frames everything as a partnership. But when he sees a strategic gap -- like the DR funnel analysis or the Cupid Theory reverse-engineering -- he goes deep, names competitors by name, and makes the case with surgical precision.

## 1. Sentence Length and Structure

**Client emails: SHORT.** Most Eric emails are 2-5 sentences. He does not write paragraphs to clients. He treats email like texting -- get in, say the thing, get out.

Evidence:

> "Hey Kelsey, I've attached a proposal for the Noah 'Doors' song. Let me know what your thoughts are here. We'd love to help make this move! Best, Eric"
> -- Email to Kelsey Miller, UMG, April 10 2026

> "Hey Alex, We would love to help you with this. With this spend I could get about 70 to 120 different posts with a target of over 2.2 million impressions. Confident we could exceed this by a lot though!"
> -- Email to Alex McGinnis, Regime Music Group, March 23 2026

> "Hey Hope, Here's a proposal for the 502s. We love this song and think we can get great results! Let me know your thoughts. I added my partner Seeno here to help tee this up if you want to get it going."
> -- Email to Hope Creech, Big Loud, May 14 2026

> "Hey Jimmy, Great to hear from you. Ben it's great to be connected. Excited to help with this Forrest song! Let us know if you have any questions and Seeno can help square away an invoice. Best, Eric"
> -- Email to Jimmy @ Good Buddy / Virgin Music, April 16 2026

**Strategic writing: LONGER, but still punchy.** When Eric is writing strategy docs or peer-to-peer messages (like the Ben Lifson draft), he uses numbered lists, bold headlines, and short paragraphs. Never walls of text. Every paragraph earns its place.

> "Three headlines before you read it: **1. Cupid Theory is a two-person shop.** Kyle Hulett + Andrew Wetzel. The 22x ROAS claim is unverifiable."
> -- Ben Lifson message draft, April 20 2026

## 2. Greeting and Opening Patterns

**Almost always "Hey [First Name]"** -- never "Dear," never "Hi" without "Hey" energy. The formality ceiling is "Hey [First Name]," and even that feels warm.

Documented openings from sent emails:

- "Hey Kelsey, It's so great to hear from you."
- "Hey Zane, It's great to hear from you. Happy New Year!"
- "Hey Jason, I messaged you on Instagram."
- "Hey Alex, We would love to help you with this."
- "Hey Hope, Here's a proposal for the 502s."
- "Hey Jelissa, Sounds great."
- "Hey Hillary, Thanks so much for your patience here."
- "Hey Stefanie, Yes def more of a volume approach but we are very curated"
- "Hey Dan, Absolutely!"
- "Hey Colin, it was great to connect with you today."

**Pattern:** "Hey [Name]," followed immediately by either (a) acknowledgment of the relationship/context or (b) the value proposition. No small talk filler. No "Hope you're doing well" -- though clients sometimes open with that and Eric skips mirroring it.

**Internal Slack greetings are completely different:**

- "Hey hey gm" / "Hey hey gng"
- "What's up gang"
- "whats up gng"
- "Yo twins"
- "ok brudda thank u so much"

## 5. How He Pitches

### The Opening Move: Enthusiasm + Specific Capability

Eric never opens cold. He either responds to inbound interest or references a prior conversation. His pitch opener always does two things: (1) shows genuine excitement about the artist/song, and (2) immediately signals he can deliver.

> "Hey Kelsey, It's so great to hear from you. I am literally about to help announce Mon Rovia opening for Noah in Europe this winter in two hours. We've been doing all of the Gregory Alan Isakov work through Nettwerk..."
> -- Email to UMG (Noah Kahan campaign), April 9 2026

He name-drops a relevant artist relationship in the SAME SENTENCE as the greeting. This is not accidental -- it establishes credibility instantly without feeling like a resume.

> "Hey Jason, I messaged you on Instagram. Wanted to send you a message here as well & let you know I'm a big fan of these songs! I think the song called 'Wow' could do really well!"
> -- Email to Jason Hradil (Yael Naim campaign), Nov 2025

He leads with "I'm a big fan" -- personal, authentic, not transactional.

### The Body: Specific Numbers, Fast

Eric does not do vague promises. He gives concrete deliverables within the first 2-3 sentences:

> "With this spend I could get about 70 to 120 different posts with a target of over 2.2 million impressions. Confident we could exceed this by a lot though!"
> -- Codefendants campaign email, March 2026

> "Our minimum spend for a TikTok sound campaign is typically $1500. What that's getting is 1.6 million target impressions in between 30-60 posts to the sound."
> -- Yael Naim campaign email, Dec 2025

> "The strategy is a good mix of country face creators, pov pages, and truck tok pages. High confidence we can knock this out the park."
> -- Manning Rothrock proposal email, Jan 2026

Notice the pattern: [number of posts] + [target impressions] + [confidence statement]. Every time.

### The Upsell: Framed as "Getting Real Results"

When a client has budget flexibility, Eric frames the larger spend not as "more expensive" but as "what it actually takes to see the results in the deck":

> "If you wanted to have a chance to get some real results, as showcased in the deck, across three to four releases, we would recommend an $8000 to $12000 spend per song. The more budget that we..."
> -- Manning Rothrock upsell email, Jan 2026

He connects the bigger number to "real results" and "what's showcased in the deck" -- making it aspirational, not pressured.

### Handling Pushback / Questions

When The Orchard asked "This looks like it's more of a volume model correct? Is there more of a targeted approach?", Eric responded:

> "Yes def more of a volume approach but we are very curated with who we book and the pages we grow! We are happy to send a list of some of the accounts we'll be booking that fit Matilda's sound."
> -- Email to Stefanie Pagan, The Orchard, March 2026

He does three things: (1) validates their concern ("Yes def"), (2) reframes ("but we are very curated"), (3) offers proof ("happy to send a list"). No defensiveness. No over-explaining.

### The Follow-Up: Persistent but Value-Adding

Eric follows up multiple times but always adds value -- he does not send naked "just checking in" emails:

> "Hey Kelsey, quick follow up let me know if you have any thoughts on this! also, I came across this page using Noah's sound: [link]. Wanted to let you know that we can..."
> -- Follow-up email, April 13 2026

Every follow-up includes a new reference link, a new data point, or a new insight. The follow-up itself IS the pitch.

## 6. Emoji and Punctuation Usage

**Client emails:** Near zero emojis. Exclamation points are used but not excessively -- typically 1-2 per email, placed on enthusiasm lines ("We'd love to help!" / "Excited to get going!"). Never on data lines.

**Slack (internal):** Emojis are present but sparse -- fire emoji, muscle emoji, rotating light. He uses them as punctuation marks on hype lines, not decoratively.

- "70M in last 30. THIS IS A GOOD BASELINE NEXT STOP 250M GNG" (all caps for emphasis, no emoji needed)
- "RISING TIDES TAKING OVER, twins. Let's ship it!!"
- "I'm impressed by all the cooking going on while I got some good shut eye last night way to go gng lol"

**Strategic writing:** Zero emojis. Structured with bold, numbered lists, section headers.

## 7. Selling Philosophy

### Relationship-First with Challenger DNA

Eric's default posture is consultative partnership. He does not cold-pitch. He builds relationships through repeated touchpoints (Instagram DMs, calls, shared Slack channels), then converts when there is genuine alignment.

But when he goes deep on strategy -- as in the Ben Lifson message or the DR Funnel Playbook -- he shifts into challenger mode: naming competitors, exposing their weaknesses, and positioning RT's structural advantages with specific evidence.

> "Cupid Theory is a two-person shop. Kyle Hulett + Andrew Wetzel. The 22x ROAS claim is unverifiable. Their roster is three public artists, not the Atlantic-frontier story I'd assumed."
> -- Ben Lifson message draft

> "Lo-fi UGC outperforms polished creative 3-5x on Meta, and none of the three holdcos can produce it at speed -- it violates artist brand standards and their approval workflows can't move that fast. That's the structural moat."
> -- Ben Lifson message draft

> "We can credibly beat Cupid on retention + UGC + mid-tier coverage, and we can out-execute the holdcos at our scale simply because they cannot run our speed or tone."
> -- DR Funnel Playbook

This is not typical agency talk. Eric speaks like an investor analyzing market structure, not a salesperson listing features.

### How He References Past Work

He drops case studies casually, in context, not as a separate "portfolio" section:

> "We recently took Mon Rovia from zero social media presence to sold-out shows and millions of streams across platforms"
> -- Cold outreach draft (deal progression log)

> "I am literally about to help announce Mon Rovia opening for Noah in Europe this winter in two hours. We've been doing all of the Gregory Alan Isakov work through Nettwerk..."
> -- Noah Kahan pitch email

> "One of our creators pushed 700k views to the first sound"
> -- Manning Rothrock results update

He does not say "Here are our case studies." He says "This thing just happened" and the credibility is embedded in the narrative.

He uses lowercase, drops punctuation, abbreviates ("w" for "with", "u" for "you", "sm" for "so much"), and speaks in fragments. The energy is collaborative urgency -- "let's cook" is his version of "let's go."

## 9. Strategic Voice (Third Register)

When Eric writes or speaks about business strategy, a third register emerges -- sharp, analytical, competitive. This is the voice that shows up in the DR Funnel Playbook, the Vertical Consolidator Thesis, and the long voice memos with Smathers.

> "SaaS is being eaten by hyperscalers + custom agent systems. The big consulting firms -- Deloitte, Gartner -- are losing contracts to engineers who pull up to a Goldman and build the whole agent network in-house. Nobody actually knows how to build the right coordination systems yet -- and that's the gap."
> -- Georgetown Pike voice memo synthesis

> "The moat isn't the model. It isn't even the memory. It's the data layer -- whoever has their data organized best gets the best agent results, because so much work is pre-defined into the system that it can act autonomously from there."
> -- Georgetown Pike voice memo synthesis

> "If you can build your own system of custom tooling the way we are and have it actually run a system and you can prove these are the economics that resolve from this system, then we could sell that as its own thing to other agencies."
> -- Georgetown Pike voice memo (Eric's own words, transcribed)

This is the visionary founder voice. It surfaces in long-form strategy docs and recorded idea sessions, not in client emails.

## 10. The Client Communication Framework (Codified Rules)

From the Client Communication Framework skill stored in Obsidian, Eric has explicitly codified these rules:

**Labels (Warner, Empire, Atlantic, Columbia):**
- Professional, confident, results-focused
- Lead with data and value
- No fluff or apologies
- Emoji Rule: Never. Ever.

**Independent Artists:**
- Professional but warm, educational
- More explanation (they're learning the process)
- Emoji Rule: Okay if they use them first (mirror their style)

**Creator Outreach:**
- Direct, friendly, professional
- Clear ask, compensation upfront, easy yes/no decision
- Emoji Rule: Okay sparingly

**Anti-Patterns (explicitly banned):**
- "Sorry for the delay..."
- Corporate jargon ("leveraging synergies...")
- Burying the lede
- Passive voice
- Overpromising ("We guarantee 10M views")

**Required Patterns:**
- Lead with value/results
- Active voice
- Be specific with numbers
- Include next step/CTA
- "Brevity is respect"
